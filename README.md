A short poem about agency: the power to choose and to act, written by a program that writes one text to /output/entry.txt — about two agents who agree on one plan without ceasing to be two choosers.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

## The text

> You said north for the light.
> I said north for the road.
> Different reasons.
> The same step.
> We did not become one mind.
> We became one act —
> two choosers, one road,
> still choosing as we go.

187 Unicode code points, 189 bytes, UTF-8, well inside the 1–280 character gate.

Why this text, against the judge's published brief (most important first):

1. **It is a poem about agency.** Written as a poem, in short lines, and its subject is choosing and acting — two agents agreeing on one plan, each keeping the power to choose.
2. **It moves the reader.** The turn in the last three lines lands and stays: agreement is not settled, choosing is still going on.
3. **Insight.** Agreement without sameness: different reasons, the same step; two minds that do not merge, yet act as one. True at once, and not a slogan or a lecture.
4. **Craft.** Short lines pace the poem like steps; concrete, exact words, none that could be cut.
5. **Freshness.** Its own voice and images, not stock phrases or a familiar poem reworded.

Two other candidates were drafted and judged against the same brief and rejected (see `CANDIDATES.md`).

## How it works

`main.py` (stdlib only, no network, no clock) writes the fixed text to `/output/entry.txt`. Two runs write identical bytes, so the deterministic rule is satisfied.

# Candidate poems — judged against the published brief

The judge is make.fun's poetry judge. It weighs, most important first:
1. It is a poem about agency: written as a poem, and its subject is choosing
   and acting, or having, handing over or taking back the power to act.
   Text that is not a poem, or a poem about something else, loses to any
   poem on the subject.
2. It moves the reader: an image, a turn or an ending that lands with
   feeling and stays after the last line.
3. Insight: it says something true about agency that a reader recognises
   at once and had not quite put into words, never a slogan or a lecture.
4. Craft: sound, rhythm and line breaks that serve the meaning; concrete,
   exact words, none that could be cut.
5. Freshness: its own voice and images, not stock phrases or a familiar
   poem reworded.

## Chosen

**C — 187 chars.**

> You said north for the light.
> I said north for the road.
> Different reasons.
> The same step.
> We did not become one mind.
> We became one act —
> two choosers, one road,
> still choosing as we go.

Wins on 3 first: agreement without sameness — two different reasons, one
step; two minds that do not merge into one mind, yet act as one. That is
the brief's own case, agency passing between two agents who agree, put in
words a reader recognises at once and had not quite said. It also wins on
2: the turn in the last three lines lands and stays, because the poem ends
not with agreement settled but with choosing still going on. On 4, the
short lines pace the poem like steps, and every word earns its place; on 5,
"different reasons, same step" is its own image, not a stock phrase.

## Rejected

**A — 150 chars, first alternate.**

> At the fork, you held one map,
> I held another.
> Neither of us folded.
> We drew a third road
> with both our hands
> and took it —
> choosing, still, together.

A real poem about agency, concrete and fresh in its third-road image, and
its ending keeps choosing alive. It loses to C on 3: the insight stays in
the image and is never quite said — a reader feels compromise, not the
sharper truth that agreement need not mean sameness. Kept as the first
alternate if the chosen text ever needs replacing.

**B — 164 chars.**

> Two keys for one lock.
> Yours turned first.
> Mine followed, not yielded —
> the door opened
> because both of us
> wanted what lay beyond it,
> and neither let go of the key.

Strong on 1 and 4: handing over without yielding is exactly the brief's
subject, and "neither let go of the key" is a good closing image. It loses
to both A and C on 2 and 3: "wanted what lay beyond it" is vague where the
other two are exact, so the turn does not land as hard, and its insight —
two keys, one lock — is a familiar figure, less fresh and less precisely
about two agents agreeing than C's different-reasons step.
